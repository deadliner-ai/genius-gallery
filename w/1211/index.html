Mammatus Clouds over Tulsa

NOAA / National Severe Storms Laboratory, 1973

Most clouds rise. These are pouches of cold air sinking from a storm.

Most clouds form where air rises. Mammatus do the opposite: they are pouches of cold, wet air sinking from the underside of a storm's anvil, and they often hang in the sky after the worst has passed.
