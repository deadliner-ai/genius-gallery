Wonder and Joy

  • Impression III (Concert)Wassily Kandinsky, 1911After a concert, Kandinsky painted the sound: a black piano, the music a yellow flood.
  • WonderlandAugust Strindberg, 1894Strindberg owned no brushes. With a knife he cut a hole of light in a dark wood: the sea.
  • Starry NightEdvard Munch, 1893Munch painted the view from the hotel where he first fell in love. Stars melt into sea.

Love

  • Love and Pain (Vampire)Edvard Munch, 1895Munch saw 'just a woman kissing a man on the neck'. A friend called it Vampire. It stuck.
  • Portrait of WallyEgon Schiele, 1912Taken from a Jewish dealer fleeing the Nazis, Wally was held in New York for 12 years.
  • Lemminkäinen's MotherAkseli Gallen-Kallela, 1897A mother holds her dead son as Mary held Jesus, waiting for a bee's honey to revive him.

The Body and Desire

  • Carcass of BeefChaïm Soutine, 1925Soutine kept a real carcass to paint until neighbours called police. Art first, he said.
  • Potsdamer PlatzErnst Ludwig Kirchner, 1914Two women on a tiny island in a tilting green square, side by side, alone in the big city.
  • Self-Portrait with the Spanish FluEdvard Munch, 1919Munch paints himself sick with the 1918 flu, open mouth echoing his famous The Scream.

Childhood

  • Dead MotherEgon Schiele, 1910A child glows inside its dead mother's dark body. Will her death doom it, or set it free?
  • Bread!Käthe Kollwitz, 1924Two hungry children pull at their mother, and there is not enough bread. Germany, 1924.
  • InheritanceEdvard Munch, 1897A mother weeps over her baby, born with syphilis. In 1897, naming it aloud was a scandal.

Solitude

  • Self-PortraitAnita Rée, 1930Anita Rée drew her quiet profile. In 1933, hounded by the Nazis, she took her own life.
  • MelancholyEdvard Munch, 1892A man pushed into a corner, head on hand, as a couple walks off to a boat. Heartbreak.
  • Evening on Karl Johan StreetEdvard Munch, 1892A crowd of pale, frozen faces walks toward you. One dark figure walks away, alone.

Faith and Doubt

  • Ecce HomoLovis Corinth, 1925Christ, bound and bleeding, between a man in white and an armoured guard: behold the man.
  • The FanaticsTyko Sallinen, 1918A prayer meeting under the cross: a woman faints, a man raises his hand. Faith as frenzy.
  • The Man of SorrowsJames Ensor, 1891Ensor took Christ's bleeding face from an old Flemish painting and gave it a demon mask.

Work and Survival

  • UnemploymentKäthe Kollwitz, 1909The mother lies with the babies, the father sits in the dark: no work, no way to help.
  • The WasherwomenMarianne von Werefkin, 1909Women bent over washtubs under flapping sheets, and a small girl watching from a doorway.
  • A Pair of ShoesVincent van Gogh, 1886Worn boots: a peasant's, said the philosopher Heidegger. The painter's, said a scholar.

Anger and Freedom

  • I Shall Gladly Endure for Art and for My Loved Ones!Egon Schiele, 1912Jailed in 1912, Schiele wrote on a drawing: 'I will gladly endure for art and loved ones'.
  • Christ's Entry into Brussels in 1889James Ensor, 1888A masked mob floods the street, and no one notices tiny Christ. He has Ensor's own face.
  • The IntrigueJames Ensor, 1890His sister wed a Chinese art dealer, and the town sneered. Here masks unmask the gossips.

Fear

  • Landscape at CéretChaïm Soutine, 1920Thick, swirling strokes make hills and houses whirl: expressive brushwork paints feeling.
  • The ScreamEdvard Munch, 1893Sky, fjord and body bend in one wave. Expressive distortion: fear reshapes the world.
  • AnxietyEdvard Munch, 1894The red sky of The Scream returns, and now a crowd of pale faces stares straight at you.

Loss

  • FarewellAugust Macke, 1914Dark, faceless figures fill a street as men leave for war in 1914. Macke's last painting.
  • The Sick ChildEdvard Munch, 1885Munch painted, scraped and repainted his dying sister for a year. The scratches remain.
  • Death in the SickroomEdvard Munch, 1893His dying sister hides in the chair facing away. The family fills the room, each alone.

Death

  • The Race Track (Death on a Pale Horse)Albert Pinkham Ryder, 1896A friend bet $500 on a horse, lost, and took his own life. Ryder painted Death riding.
  • Dance of Death 1809Albin Egger-Lienz, 1908Death leads with a spade, four farmers with clubs in step behind: the old dance of death.
  • The Nameless OnesAlbin Egger-Lienz, 1916'To the Nameless': soldiers bent low under fire, body after body, none of them named.

The Universe

  • Artemis IIThe first crew to the Moon since 1972. In 2026 they flew farther from Earth than anyone.
  • Stephen HawkingHe showed black holes aren't fully black: they leak faint radiation and slowly evaporate.
  • The SunOur star, powered by fusion. Its light takes 8 minutes to reach us, so we see its past.

Nature

  • David AttenboroughPresenting nature on TV since 1954, in old age he became a leading voice for saving it.
  • WeatherUneven sun heating stirs the air into weather, too chaotic to forecast past 2 weeks.
  • Chicxulub craterThe asteroid that ended the dinosaurs' era left a 200 km crater in Mexico, now buried.

Science

  • ChatGPTA chatbot you talk to in plain words. It got 100 million users in 2 months, a record then.
  • Albert EinsteinHe showed that time and space bend. Yet his Nobel was for work on light, not relativity.
  • Artificial IntelligenceMachines doing work that needs thinking. Its 1956 founders hoped for a leap in one summer.

Technology and Inventions

  • Sinking of the TitanicTitanic, 1912: lifeboats for barely half on board, yet more than the law required.
  • Goiânia accidentBrazil, 1987: glowing blue powder from a dumped cancer machine was radioactive. Four died.
  • Airbus A380The biggest airliner ever, two full decks, cleared for 853 people. Only 251 were built.

Psychology

  • The Dunning-Kruger EffectThe weakest scorers thought they beat 62% of people. It may be partly a statistical quirk.
  • ADHDNot laziness: a brain that struggles to aim focus and curb impulses. Noted back in 1798.
  • Borderline Personality DisorderEmotions that hit like storms and a fierce fear of being left. With therapy, most improve.

Philosophy

  • PhilosophyAsking by reason alone what exists, what we can know, how to live. Greek: love of wisdom.
  • Noam ChomskyKids learn speech from little input, so he said grammar is partly inborn. Many dispute it.
  • SocratesHe wrote nothing. He questioned people until their certainty cracked. Athens executed him.

History

  • NapoleonNot short: at about 1.68 m Napoleon was average for his day. British cartoons shrank him.
  • Vietnam WarNorth vs South, the US backing the South. Up to 3 million Vietnamese died.
  • Henry VIIITo annul his first marriage, he broke with Rome and made himself head of England's Church.

Society

  • Queen VictoriaHer 63-year reign named an era. Her children wed into Europe's royal and noble houses.
  • Adolf HitlerHe took power without a majority, then led Germany into World War II and the Holocaust.
  • Barack ObamaSon of a Kenyan and an 18-year-old from Kansas, he became the first Black U.S. president.

Culture

  • CleopatraLast queen of Egypt's Greek dynasty, and the first of its rulers to learn Egyptian.
  • World War II1939 to 1945, nearly every country involved, 60 to 75 million dead. Most were civilians.
  • OdysseyOdysseus's ten-year voyage home, while his wife unweaves each night to stall her suitors.

Language and Words

  • Spanish languageSpanish has 519 million native speakers, more than English, more in Mexico than in Spain.
  • Google TranslateGoogle Translate first learned from UN documents, and for most pairs it went via English.
  • Japanese language123 million speak Japanese, yet no tie to any language outside Japan's islands is proven.

Architecture

  • Sagrada Família, BarcelonaGaudí's columns branch like trees, so the nave feels like a forest of stone.
  • The Twin Towers, New YorkElevators ran like the subway: express to a 'sky lobby', then local, freeing floor space.
  • Taj MahalA tomb for a wife who died in childbirth. Its minarets tilt out, to fall away from her.

Economics

  • Karl MarxMarx said capitalism would collapse from its own conflicts, between owners and workers.
  • TheranosValued at $9 billion for a finger-prick blood test that didn't work as claimed.
  • Human Development IndexRanks countries by lifespan, schooling and income together: people, not just money.

Personal Growth

  • Henry David ThoreauJailed for refusing a tax that funded slavery and war. His essay on it inspired Gandhi.
  • YogaAn Indian discipline built on meditation. The pose-based yoga most know is largely modern.
  • SenecaEmperor Nero's tutor, a philosopher: life isn't short, it's long enough if not wasted.
