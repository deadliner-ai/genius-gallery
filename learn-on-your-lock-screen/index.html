Learn something every time you unlock your phone

You unlock your phone dozens of times a day, and most of those seconds go to nothing. Genius Gallery puts one image and one short line on your lock screen instead: a masterpiece, a discovery, an idea. Inspiration, not notification.

What a lock screen line looks like

  • The Starry NightVincent van Gogh, 1889His asylum window showed no village. He painted one in, asleep under a wild sky.
  • Mona LisaLeonardo da Vinci, 1503No outlines at her mouth and eyes, only a soft blur, sfumato. So the smile never settles.
  • Survivorship BiasWarplanes came back with no holes in some spots: planes hit there never returned at all.
  • Tacoma Narrows BridgeIn a moderate wind the deck began to twist, each twist feeding the next, until it tore.
  • PlaceboExpecting to heal can help the body, even with a sugar pill. Some gain knowing it's fake.
  • Pale Blue DotNASA / Voyager 1, 1990That speck in the sunbeam is Earth. On it, an astronomer wrote, everyone you love lived.

How it works

  1. Install Genius Gallery from Google Play and tap Start.
  2. From then on, a new work with one line about it waits on your lock screen every time you unlock.
  3. Curious? Tap for the full story and Wikipedia. Mark what you love, and the app learns your taste on your phone.

Not flashcards

Lock screen vocabulary apps drill you on words. This is different: no quizzes, no streaks, nothing to remember. Just one thing worth knowing, from 24 collections that run from Van Gogh and Hokusai to the universe, psychology, history and philosophy.

Get it on Google Play

Free, with no ads and no account. Every line is written to make sense without background knowledge; if one doesn't, tell us at support@deadliner.ai.
